\section{Purpose}
The primary purpose of this project, initiated by a leading Bike Association,
is to develop and deploy an advanced support system for the cycling community designed
to thrive within a transport landscape dominated by cars.

The core idea is to foster
a collaborative digital environment where cyclists can actively record, consult,
and publish both their own and other users' riding paths.

This platform will serve as the definitive centralized resource for finding highly recommended routes tailored
precisely to the user's specific experience, preferences, and safety considerations.

Ultimately, the system aims to significantly enhance the quality of the cycling experience
by providing verified, community-rated routes, thereby connecting riders and making
high-quality path discovery universally accessible

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{|l|X|}
\hline
\textbf{MP} & \textbf{Description} \\ \hline
MP1 & The system guesses that the user is biking by analyzing the detected speed \\ \hline
MP2 & The system computes statistics related to a trip (e.g., total distance covered, average speed, performance metrics) \\ \hline
MP3 & The system identifies potential anomalies (e.g., potholes) by processing raw data from the device sensors (accelerometer and gyroscope) \\ \hline
MP4 & The system computes the score of a path based on its status and its effectiveness in reaching the destination \\ \hline
MP5 & The system merges path information incoming from multiple users, evaluating data freshness and the number of confirmations received \\ \hline
MP6 & The system stores trip history and path information in the database for future retrieval \\ \hline

\subsection{Document Structure}
This document is organized into four main chapters, as outlined below:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 1 - Introduction:} Provides an overview of the BBP (Best Bike Paths) system, defining its goals, the scope of the project, and the terminology used. It also lists the reference documents and the domain phenomena (World, Machine, and Shared).
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 2 - Overall Description:} Offers a high-level perspective on the product. It includes the product perspective through scenarios, the domain model represented by a Class Diagram, and State Diagrams to describe the trip recording logic. It also lists user characteristics and the domain assumptions.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 3 - Specific Requirements:} This is the core of the document. It details the functional requirements through Use Case diagrams and descriptions. It also defines the non-functional requirements (performance, safety, security, etc.) and the external interface requirements (User, Hardware, and Software interfaces).
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 4 - Formal Analysis using Alloy:} Presents the formal modeling of the system using the Alloy language. It includes the signatures, facts, and assertions used to verify the consistency of the domain model and the core logic of trip registration.
\end{itemize}
